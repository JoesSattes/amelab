Good AI is AI we understand.

Like a bonsai, a model can only be shaped well by someone who knows how it grows. We are an open, non-commercial lab that studies how AI models are built, so they can be made smaller, fairer and safe to live with.

L1 · Model Anatomy & Control

Every model grows like a tree you can read.

This is Qwen3-0.6B, drawn from its real config. The roots are its vocabulary. The trunk carries 28 rings, one per decoder block. The crown is the output head, which shares the roots' rows. The light climbing the trunk is a token passing through.

L2 · On-device AI · The vocabulary tax

Look underground: the roots outweigh any branch.

151,936 rows of 1,024 numbers each. That one matrix holds about as many parameters as ten decoder blocks, and a phone has to load all of it.

155.6Membedding parameters≈ 10×one decoder block

L2 · Pruning with a certificate

A Thai assistant never uses most of those roots. So we prune them, and certify the cut.

Roots for scripts a user will never type are cut away. Only the roots that feed the chip stay. Before anything ships, a certificate states what the cut cost in quality.

L2 · On the device

Pruned well, it fits in your pocket.

A smaller model loads faster, uses less memory and runs inside an app or on a phone. What you say to it can stay on the device.

L3 · Human–AI Interaction

Living with it: AI you can talk to, trust and stop.

Our next study looks at voice assistants that run on the phone: how people speak to them, when they trust them, and how easily they can interrupt them. Thai speech in real rooms comes first.

L4 · Multilingual Equity

Every language grows its own leaves. Southeast Asia first.

Most models are built around English and priced in its tokens. We measure what each language pays and test every method on Southeast Asian languages first, then carry it to the rest of the world.

FAQ

Is the lab a company?

No. It is an open, non-commercial research project. Nothing we publish is sold.

Can I use the data in my product?

Each dataset states its license on Hugging Face. [license summary]

Why Southeast Asia first?

Its languages pay some of the highest token costs and get the least testing. A method that holds here holds widely.

How do I cite the lab?

Cite the paper you used: arXiv:2611.19913 for the Vocabulary Tax, doi:10.2139/ssrn.7699619 for the Manifest census.

One email when we publish

New papers, tools and datasets. No more than once a month.
